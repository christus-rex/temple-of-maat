# Pistis Sophia — The Descent and Return

This named Temple pilgrimage complements **Enoch — The Angelic Mirror** without reusing Enoch's eight-gate symbolic architecture. It follows the source corpus's own stronger organizing number: **thirteen**.

## Source basis and authority boundary

The route is grounded in three project sources:

1. **G. R. S. Mead, _Pistis Sophia: A Gnostic Miscellany_ (1921)** — a public-domain English witness to the Askew Codex tradition, British Library Add MS 5114. Mead remains historically useful but is not a modern critical translation. The internal composition of the work has been debated, including older proposals about whether some later repentances are secondary.
2. **_Pistis Sophia — The Arithmetic of the Returning Light_ (2026)** — a modern Temple study that explicitly describes its thirteen-station sequence as an original contemporary retelling, not a line-by-line ancient ritual program. Its Greek isopsephy and digital-root interpretations are disclosed contemplative methods rather than proof of ancient encoding.
3. **_Pistis Sophia — Faith-Wisdom, Repentance, and the Ascent of Light_ (2026)** — a researched interpretive companion that separates ancient claims from modern evidence and frames symbolic applications as interpretation.

The route therefore never presents its modern station titles or psychological applications as original instructions from the Askew Codex.

## Why thirteen

The Pistis Sophia narrative presents **thirteen repentances**. In the Temple companions, that cycle is read as a broad movement through awakening, naming, responsibility, resistance, persistence, acceptance, rescue, restoration, and return. The source companion explicitly warns that the sequence is not a magic formula and that the ancient text does not assign a universal psychological meaning to every number.

The route uses that textual thirteen as an organizing scaffold while clearly labeling its contemporary retelling as **MODERN** and its private application as **PERSONAL**.

## Thirteen stations

1. **The Glimpse** — longing without premature certainty.
2. **The Desire** — the need beneath the desired object.
3. **The Counterfeit Radiance** — reality, freedom, compassion, and repair as tests of apparent light.
4. **The Descent** — accountability without turning history into identity.
5. **The Loss of Power** — measurable effects on agency, body, work, money, sleep, relationships, and choice.
6. **The First Cry** — truthful speech before spiritual performance.
7. **The Delay** — perseverance without romanticizing preventable harm.
8. **The Mocking Powers** — correction without contempt.
9. **The Witnesses** — testimony held in a community that can listen and challenge.
10. **The Turning** — metanoia made visible through proportionate repair and changed conduct.
11. **The Two Powers of Help** — compassion and structure working together to restore capacity.
12. **The Restoration** — recovered power measured by agency and service, not revenge or spectacle.
13. **The Commission** — the proof of ascent is conduct among others.

## Private Record of the Returning Light

The route uses five device-local fields:

`What drew me downward → What I mistook for light → What was lost → What I recognized → What wisdom returns with me`

This record is designed to preserve both dignity and accountability. It does not require the visitor to deny personal spiritual meaning, but it does require interpretation to remain distinguishable from observation, evidence, consequence, and conduct.

State is stored under:

`temple_pilgrimage_pistis_sophia_v1`

It uses the shared state contract:

`temple-of-maat/pilgrimage-state-v1`

The route never writes personal records into the public Knowledge Kernel, Relationship Graph, Library catalog, or chamber canon. Export occurs only through a deliberate local JSON download.

## Discernment covenant

The route's central test follows the project's modern Pistis Sophia studies:

- **Reality** — does the insight remain coherent when checked?
- **Freedom** — does it increase agency rather than dependency?
- **Compassion** — does it enlarge care beyond ego or faction?
- **Repair** — does it lead to concrete right action when harm has occurred?

The route also preserves the project's wider requirements: symbolic or visionary claims are not automatically empirical facts; numerical correspondence is not proof of doctrine or divine intention; and practice is judged by clarity, harmlessness, wisdom, compassion, responsibility, freedom from coercion, and real-world follow-through.

## Safety and anti-distortion rules

This pilgrimage does not teach that pain must continue before help is deserved. It does not treat sleep loss, danger, coercion, financial deterioration, isolation, or loss of reality-testing as evidence of spiritual progress. Practical and professional forms of help remain compatible with spiritual practice.

Recovered power is not permission to reproduce the counterfeit. The final station therefore refuses spiritual claims used to demand submission, sexual access, money, secrecy, exceptional treatment, or exemption from accountability. Its completion practice is a thirteen-line rule of life in which every line must describe an observable behavior rather than a title or metaphysical claim.

## Integration with the Temple

The route is a progressive enhancement layered inside the existing Journey interface. It must preserve:

- the manual Temple threshold;
- the existing 72-chamber Pilgrim Journey;
- Enoch route state and behavior;
- device-local private records;
- no ritual-media autoplay;
- mobile readability at 360 px and 412 px;
- source / modern interpretation / personal application distinctions;
- no personal-record leakage into public research data.

The intended axis is complementary rather than hierarchical:

`ENOCH — SOURCE → VISION → DISCERNMENT → VERIFICATION → CONDUCT`

`PISTIS SOPHIA — DESCENT → RECOGNITION → TURNING → RESTORATION → RETURN`

Both routes end in the same Temple measure: knowledge becomes wisdom when it changes conduct.
